Nexpill keeps track of every dose for the people you care for: when it was given, when the next one can be, and a reminder on the minute when it's due. It was written by a caregiver, for caregivers, and it does all of that on your phone.

<b>The one thing to know, at a glance</b>
Each medication says where it stands right now: due in 8 minutes, overdue by 25, can be given again at 8:19. What needs doing first is at the top, with a button to log the dose. Nexpill does the time arithmetic, across midnight and clock changes, and asks before you log a dose early.

<b>Every kind of schedule</b>
• Every few hours, including odd intervals like 4 h 45 min
• At set times of day
• As needed, with a minimum gap between doses
• Tapers whose interval changes by date

<b>Reminders that arrive on time</b>
Exact alarms held by the phone itself, so they come on the minute: with no signal, in airplane mode, after a restart. Mark a dose given or snooze it straight from the notification. For the doses that can't be missed, a reminder can ring like an alarm clock until someone deals with it.

<b>A record you can trust</b>
Fix a mistaken time with a correction that keeps the original. Note who gave each dose. Track how much is left. Save the history as a spreadsheet, or a plain summary for the doctor.

<b>Your records stay on your phone</b>
• No account, no server, no sync
• No internet permission: Android itself stops Nexpill from sending anything anywhere
• No analytics, advertising or tracking, and excluded from Android's cloud backup
• Back up to a file wherever you choose

Nexpill tracks timing. It doesn't give medical advice, recommend doses or check interactions.

Nexpill is free and open source under the MIT licence, with no ads or premium tier, and it always will be.
