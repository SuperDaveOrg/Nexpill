Installing

  1. Tap Download preview APK on your phone. Chrome warns that the file might be harmful, as it does for every app from outside the Play Store. Tap Download anyway.
  2. Open the download and tap Install (or Update, if you already have Nexpill). The first time, your phone may ask you to allow Chrome to install unknown apps.
  3. If you see App blocked to protect your device, tap More details, then Install anyway.
  4. Open Nexpill and allow notifications, so you can check the reminders too.

A preview installs over the released app and keeps its records, and the next release installs over the preview. A preview can have bugs the release doesn't: if you use Nexpill for real medications, save Back up to a file in Settings before installing.

What to check

About half an hour, at your own pace. Use made-up people and medications, never anyone's real ones. Nexpill keeps everything on the phone, and you can delete it all afterwards in Settings.

1. Switch to your language

Nexpill follows the phone's language. To pick one just for Nexpill, go to Settings → Language. Check that name and the menu read naturally.

2. Add a pretend person

Add someone you care for, then a medication or two. Try each kind of schedule: every few hours, at set times, as needed, and a taper. Read every label and hint.

3. Watch the statuses change

Give a dose, then look again later: "next dose at", "due soon", "due now", "overdue". These are the words that matter most; see the table below.

4. Reminders

In Settings, Send a test reminder. Then let a real one arrive and use its buttons. Reminders are read half-asleep, so they must be short and clear.

5. History and corrections

Open a medication's dose history. Correct an entry's time, skip a dose, delete one. Check the wording of every question the app asks before it changes something.

6. What leaves the phone

Make the printable medication summary and save the dose history as a spreadsheet. Someone else, perhaps a doctor, will read these in your language.

Beyond wrong words, tell us about anything that sounds machine-made, too formal or too casual, text that's cut off or doesn't fit, and places that are still in English. (The name Nexpill, and anything you typed yourself, stay as they are.)

The words that must be exact

A tired caregiver who reads "due" where the app means "can be given", or "missed" where it means "overdue", may give a dose at the wrong time. Please check that your language keeps each of these meanings apart, everywhere it appears.

In EnglishWhat it must mean
due nowThe dose should be given now.
due soon / due in 8 minNot yet; it will be due shortly.
next dose at… / too earlyNot yet; giving it now would be early.
can be givenFor as-needed medications: allowed now, never required.
overdueIt was due and hasn't been given.
missedSo late that the next dose is nearly due.
skipDeliberately not given; the schedule carries on.
correctionReplaces a mistaken entry, which stays in the history.

Nexpill never gives medical advice, in any language. If a sentence reads like it's telling someone what to take or how much, that's a mistake worth reporting.
